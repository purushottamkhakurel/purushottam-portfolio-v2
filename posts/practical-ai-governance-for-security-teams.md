# Practical AI governance starts with knowing where AI is used

Artificial intelligence is becoming part of everyday work. Teams use AI tools to summarize information, draft content, analyze data and support decisions. These uses can save time, but they also create questions about privacy, accuracy, access and accountability.

For security and GRC teams, AI governance does not need to begin with a complicated framework. It can begin with a simple question: **Where is AI being used, and what information is going into it?**

## Build a clear inventory

Organizations cannot manage AI-related risk if they do not know which tools and systems are in use.

Start by identifying approved AI services, embedded AI features in existing software and any internal models or workflows. Record the business purpose, owner, data involved and whether the tool makes or influences important decisions.

This inventory gives security, legal, privacy and business teams a shared starting point.

## Understand the data

Not all information should be entered into every AI tool. Teams should define what data can be used, what must be restricted and which services are approved for sensitive information.

Consider customer data, personal information, credentials, confidential business material and regulated records. Employees need practical guidance they can follow—not only a policy hidden in a document library.

## Keep a human accountable

AI output can be incomplete, inaccurate or misleading. It may sound confident while missing important context.

For higher-impact work, people should verify outputs before relying on them. The organization should also make clear who is responsible for decisions supported by AI. A tool can assist a process, but responsibility should not disappear into automation.

## Check vendors and ongoing changes

When a third-party service includes AI capabilities, ask how data is handled, whether it is used for model training, how access is controlled, what subprocessors are involved and how changes are communicated.

AI features can change over time. Vendor reviews should therefore be revisited when the service, data use or risk profile changes—not treated as a one-time exercise.

## Make governance practical

A useful starting checklist is:

- Maintain an inventory of AI tools and use cases.
- Define approved and prohibited data types.
- Assign an owner to each meaningful use case.
- Require human review for important outputs.
- Assess third-party AI providers.
- Review incidents, changes and performance regularly.

**My takeaway:** Responsible AI adoption is not about blocking every new tool. It is about understanding the use, protecting the data, checking the output and keeping clear human accountability.
